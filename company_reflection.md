# Company Overview & Reflection

## Why do you think Focus Bear was created?

From my perspective, Focus Bear was created due to personal experience of the developers of the issues of current productivity systems. According to the manifesto, the company's team is AuDHD and the company's founders have already used many productivity apps and systems but most of them made them feel worse. The founder's note is saying the same - the founder has already tried to use many apps but all of them were assuming something about him that was not true especially in the morning.

Therefore, the company has been developed in order to create an alternative kind of productivity app. Instead of offering yet another blank to-do list or relying on user's willpower, it creates structures in the form of guided routines, distraction blocking, focus sessions, and break reminders. On the website, it is described as reducing decisions and guiding users step-by-step.

## What problem is Focus Bear solving?

Focus Bear is solving several interconnected problems that are making people unable to work productively consistently.

The first challenge is **digital distraction**. Focus Bear allows the user to block distracting websites and applications on Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android. There are configurable block lists and allow lists; the application can also ask the user whether access to a certain website or application is necessary. In Grizzly Bear mode, everything that has not been explicitly allowed gets blocked right away. Finally, there is also a "break glass" emergency override in case blocking was done in error.

The second problem is **decision fatigue and difficulty following routines**. Instead of presenting an entire routine at once, Focus Bear guides the user through each activity in a routine separately. The user can create his/her morning and night routines or choose from recommended habit packs. The website specifically states that this is to reduce the need to repeatedly decide what needs to be done next.

The third problem is **working for too long without taking breaks**. The manifesto recognises that hyperfocus can become detrimental to wellbeing. Focus Bear includes break reminders, hyperfocus detection, ability to customise break activities, and meeting detection to prevent the former from popping up during the call.

The app also provides these features between different devices. Starting the deep work session on a computer, for instance, will automatically put the user's phone in Zen mode, restricting access to distracting applications during the session.

## Why do you think this mission is important?

I think the mission of Focus Bear is important because it does not regard focus solely as something that requires harder work or more discipline. Its manifesto notes that the issue is not lack of discipline but rather overload of attention that needs to be channelled, not fought against.

I also think that the fact of focusing on sustainable productivity is important. The manifesto mentions explicitly that deep work should not interfere with wellbeing and that the user needs to take breaks, rest and recover. It is seen in the features of the app: reminders about breaks, activities of movement and stretching, customisable break activities, meetings detection.

The fact that Focus Bear focuses on empathy and not shame is another feature. The company claims that its tools are gentle and respectful to the way the mind works of real people and is not some kind of a strict productivity method that makes users feel that they have failed when they cannot consistently follow a rigid system.

Lastly, the company claims that its approach is based on evidence rather than promises and that its features are evidence-based. According to the manifesto, the company has conducted a randomised controlled trial to measure the effect of the product.

## How does Focus Bear's work align with your personal values or interests?

The work of Focus Bear aligns with my interest in software engineering since it is a good example of creating software to address particular user problems. Since I am a frontend developer, it is interesting for me how the application's design increases or reduces the amount of cognitive load needed for the user.

For example, the application uses the approach of showing one routine activity at a time. This approach is more specific than the idea of using a habit checklist. Also, the distraction-blocking feature does not only use the approach of showing statistics on how long the user spent on the screens but also intervenes by blocking websites and applications or asking the user if he or she really wants to use the blocked websites. It is interesting for me since the design should somehow communicate this to the user without making the application annoying or difficult to use.

Finally, my interest in the topic of software reliability and user trust is also linked to this task because blocking functions of Focus Bear influence directly what users have access to on their devices, therefore, it is critical for blocking process to operate reliably and for the user to understand the nature of blocking and emergency override. Also, according to the information provided by the website, the application does not save keystrokes and screenshots made by the user, thus, privacy boundaries should be communicated.

As a person focused on making the user experience better, I could understand why the mentioned values like empathy, intuitive design and reduction of unnecessary decision making are important in designing and testing frontend features.

## Do you personally relate to any of the challenges that Focus Bear aims to solve?

Yes. First of all, being a final year software engineering student, I can definitely relate to the problem of digital distractions while studying or working on some technical task. In this case, websites, notifications or any other unrelated application can easily distract me from my main task.

Another area that I can relate to is managing multiple responsibilities simultaneously and prioritising which task should be done first. That is how I can understand the idea behind the guided routines provided by Focus Bear and its step-by-step approach instead of providing a large list of steps and expecting a user to organise everything himself/herself.

The problem of sustainable focus is relevant to long-term sessions in technical work. Working on a programming problem for hours, a person may lose track of the time he/she spent on this activity. Breaks and the necessity to avoid any negative consequences of hyperfocus can be understood from my personal experience in studying and working with programming.

These are also the spheres where I will be able to make contributions in my internship. With my work on frontend problems, testing of the application, finding of confusing interactions and making the interface more understandable, I will help to make these particular features easier to understand and use.
